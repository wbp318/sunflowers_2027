# The Delta's First Sunflower Delivery Point
### Proposal · Viserion Grain — Lake Providence, LA · August 2026

A low-capital program that puts fee income on this facility's slack late-summer window, builds grower originations in East Carroll Parish, and holds a barge card no competitor can match.

|---|---|---|

## 2 · Sunflowers grow here. Nobody buys them here.

The nearest buyer of any kind is ~600 road miles away. That single gap — no local delivery point — is what keeps a workable crop out of the Delta. **Whoever becomes the delivery point owns the origination.**

| Nearest buyers (from Lake Providence) | Road miles |
|---|---:|
| Red River Commodities — Lubbock, TX (birdseed) | ~600 |
| Lamar, CO (oil crush) | ~750 |
| ADM Enderlin / Cargill West Fargo, ND (main crush) | ~1,100 |

## 3 · The facility already has every piece the program needs

| Already in place | What the program uses it for |
|---|---|
| Truck receiving, scales, grading | Delivery point for growers within ~50 miles |
| Grain dryers | Sunflower stores at 10% moisture (7–8% for summer carry) |
| Segregated bins | Identity-preserved high-oleic; one 100k-bu bin covers the pilot |
| Truck loadout | 500-cwt loads to Lubbock or NSA-directory buyers |
| **Barge loadout on the Mississippi** | Export/Gulf option at ~¼ the truck freight — nobody else has it |
| Merchandising desk | Basis and freight arbitrage once volume proves out |
| Aug–Sept slack window | Early-planted Delta sunflowers harvest *before* the corn/soybean rush |

The only genuinely new operational requirement: **daily dryer housekeeping**. Sunflower fines are the documented fire risk (NDSU) — a cleaning discipline, not a capital project.

---

## 4 · Three ways in — start where the risk is zero

**A — Toll handling · 2027 · RECOMMENDED.** Growers hold act-of-God contracts directly with the buyer. Viserion never owns the seed — it charges receiving, drying, storage, and freight-coordination fees. Risk: near zero. Return: fee income on idle capacity.

**B — Merchandising · 2028.** Viserion posts a local bid, owns the seed, sells delivered-Lubbock or via barge, keeps basis + freight margin on top of fees. Adds historically $0.20–0.50/cwt — in exchange for market risk in a thin, one-buyer market.

**C — Birdseed line JV · 2029, evaluate.** At ≥5,000 acres, add cleaning/bagging at the elevator — bagged black-oil birdseed and deer-plot seed sold regionally, erasing the freight penalty entirely. Capital $150–500k placeholder, pending written vendor quotes.

## 5 · Viserion economics — fee income scales cleanly

**Toll fee stack, $/cwt:** receiving 0.45 + drying 0.50 + storage 0.30 + freight coordination 0.10 = **1.35 revenue** − 0.55 variable cost = **~$0.80 contribution**.

| | Pilot · 2,000 ac | Scale · 5,000 ac | Target · 10,000 ac |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Volume (16 cwt/ac) | 32,000 cwt | 80,000 cwt | 160,000 cwt |
| Truckloads | 64 | 160 | 320 |
| **Contribution** | **$25,600** | **$64,000** | **$128,000** |

Against $25–75k one-time setup → **payback in 1–3 seasons even at pilot scale.** Structure B merchandising margin comes on top.

## 6 · Grower economics — what recruits the acres

Farmgate = $22.75/cwt delivered − $3.60 freight − $1.35 fees = **$17.80/cwt**. High-oleic pays +2% per oil point above 40% — 45% oil adds ~10% to price for free.

| Scenario | Net $/acre before land |
|---|---:|
| Dryland · 1,600 lb | **+$35** |
| Dryland + 45% oil premium | **+$71** |
| Irrigated · 2,200 lb + premium | **+$157** |

Honest read: base-case dryland loses to a good soybean year. Sunflower recruits acres as a rotation / marginal-ground play, plus the premium and irrigation. Minimum viable program: **~2,000 contracted acres (≈64 loads).**

---

## 7 · The barge card

If an export or Gulf-side buyer materializes, Lake Providence loads barges at roughly a quarter of the truck cost — and only a river elevator can offer that.

| Outbound lane | $/cwt |
|---|---:|
| Truck → Lubbock (base case) | $3.60 |
| Barge → Gulf (Viserion-only option) | ~$1.00 |

**Worth ~$42/acre to growers** — it turns a marginal crop into a solid one.

## 8 · Where it breaks — and what we do about it

**Grower net $/ac (dryland, 16 cwt, no premium):**

| Delivered price | Truck ($3.60) | Barge ($1.00) |
|---|---:|---:|
| $20.00 | **−$9** | +$33 |
| $22.75 (current) | +$35 | +$77 |
| $25.00 | +$71 | +$113 |
| $28.00 | +$119 | +$161 |

Prices moved $16.60→$31.40/cwt in 2021. Act-of-God contracts locked before planting are non-negotiable.

**The honest case against — and mitigations:**
- **One-buyer risk** — program realistically sells to Red River Lubbock first. → Signed offtake before any bin commits; NSA directory for a second outlet.
- **Thin grower math** at base case. → Oil premium, irrigated acres, rotation ground.
- **Freight is an extrapolation** — a $4.50/cwt spot quote breaks dryland. → Go/no-go gate.
- **Insurance** must sign off on the dryer fire protocol. → Early underwriter conversation.
- **Small headline dollars** for a Pinnacle-backed platform. → The pitch is zero-risk fees, slack-window utilization, originations, and barge option value.
