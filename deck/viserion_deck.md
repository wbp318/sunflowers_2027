# The Delta's First Sunflower Delivery Point
### Proposal · Viserion Grain — Lake Providence, LA (East Carroll Parish) · August 2026

A low-capital program that puts fee income on this facility's slack late-summer window, builds grower originations in East Carroll Parish, and holds a barge card no competitor can match. Structured so Viserion carries **zero price risk in year one**.

| **$25–75k** setup | **~$0.80/cwt** contribution | **$128k/yr** at scale | **0** price risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| one-time: screens, segregation protocol, dryer procedure, spares | toll model: $1.35 fees − $0.55 variable | contribution at 10,000 ac, before merchandising margin | year one: growers hold AOG contracts; Viserion never owns the seed |

*Prepared by William, Tap Parker Farms — builds on a fact-checked multi-source feasibility study (July 2026: 22 sources, 76 claims extracted, 25 adversarially verified; refuted claims excluded). Full model: [VISERION_PLAN.md](../VISERION_PLAN.md).*

## 2 · Sunflowers grow here. Nobody buys them here.

The northeast Louisiana Delta grows sunflowers well — but the nearest buyer of any kind is ~600 road miles away, and the main U.S. crush capacity is 1,100+ miles north. No local delivery point exists anywhere in the region. That single gap keeps a workable crop out of the Delta — **whoever closes it owns the origination.**

| Every plausible buyer (from Lake Providence) | Road miles | Type |
|---|---:|---|
| Red River Commodities — Lubbock, TX | ~600 | birdseed / wildlife food (closest plausible buyer) |
| Lamar, CO | ~750 | oil crush |
| ADM Enderlin / Cargill West Fargo, ND | ~1,100 | main U.S. crush capacity |
| CHS Fargo, ND | ~1,100 | confection kernel & inshell roasting |

- **Market-creation play, not plug-in:** nobody serves this region — that is both the risk and the moat.
- The **NSA buyer directory** (sunflowernsa.com/buyers, 15 categories) is the tool for lining up second outlets.
- Growers within ~50 miles — East Carroll, Madison, West Carroll parishes plus SE Arkansas and the Mississippi side — currently have no reason to plant a single acre.

*Sources: AgMRC sunflower profile · redriv.com facility list · chsinc.com · NSA buyer directory. Full citations in [REPORT.md](../REPORT.md).*

## 3 · The facility already has every piece the program needs

Lake Providence is one of the 11 river elevators Viserion acquired from Zen-Noh (ex-Bunge) in July 2021 — a working Mississippi River terminal with truck receiving and barge loadout. The sunflower program is an *overlay* on existing assets, not a build.

| Already in place | What the program uses it for |
|---|---|
| Truck receiving, scales, grading | Delivery point for growers within ~50 miles; standard FM/moisture docking applies |
| Grain dryers | Sunflower must reach 10% moisture for winter storage, 7–8% for summer carry (Louisiana heat makes NDSU's numbers conservative minimums) |
| Segregated bins | Identity-preserved high-oleic; light test weight (28–32 lb/bu) means one ~100k-bu bin holds ~30,000 cwt — the entire pilot |
| Truck loadout | 500-cwt (50,000 lb) loads; sunflower weight-limits before it cubes out |
| **Barge loadout on the Mississippi** | Export/Gulf option at ~$0.75–1.00/cwt vs ~$3.60 truck — an edge no landlocked competitor can copy |
| Merchandising desk | Basis and freight arbitrage among Lubbock, crushers, and barge/export once volume proves out |
| Aug–Sept slack window | Early-planted Delta sunflowers harvest *before* the corn/soybean rush — fees land in an otherwise quiet month |

**The one new operational requirement: fines discipline.** Sunflower hairs/fibers rubbed off in handling are the documented dryer-fire hazard. NDSU (Hellevang): *"the fire hazard is not related to drying temperature, but rather to housekeeping and managing the dryer."* The control is **daily dryer cleanout** plus dust management at legs and transfer points — a written procedure and a checklist, not a capital project. Insurance underwriter review is Gate 3.

## 5 · Three ways in — start where the risk is zero

### A — Toll handling (fee-for-service) · 2027 · RECOMMENDED OPENER
- Growers sign act-of-God contracts *directly with the end buyer* before planting
- Viserion never takes title — charges receiving, drying, storage, freight-coordination fees ($1.35/cwt stack)
- **Viserion risk:** near zero (operational only) · **Return:** ~$0.80/cwt contribution on idle capacity
- **Commitment needed:** fee schedule + one segregated bin + drying protocol

### B — Merchandising · 2028, after quality history
- Viserion posts a local bid, owns the seed, sells delivered-Lubbock / barge / crushers
- Keeps basis + freight margin on top of fees — historically **$0.20–0.50/cwt** where elevators arbitrage sunflower buyers (K-State)
- **Caveat:** the classic arbitrage model assumed several reachable buyers; here it starts as one — that's why B waits for year two and a second outlet

### C — Birdseed / deer-plot bagging JV · 2029, evaluate at ≥5,000 ac
- Cleaning + screening + bagging line at or near the elevator (a birdseed line omits the costly dehull/polish/color-sort stages of a confection line)
- Bagged black-oil birdseed + deer-plot seed sold to regional feed stores, co-ops, hunters — the Delta is prime deer country
- **Every bag sold locally erases the whole $3.60/cwt freight penalty**
- Capital $150–500k placeholder — vendor quotes required first; simplest food-safety burden (not human food)

**Sequence: A → B → C.** Prove the crop and buyer with zero risk, add merchandising margin once quality history exists, bring processing local only when volume justifies it.

## 7 · Grower economics — what recruits the acres

Farmgate = $22.75 delivered − $3.60 freight − $1.35 fees = **$17.80/cwt**. High-oleic pays +2% per oil point above 40% — Delta agronomy at 44–46% oil adds ~10% to price at no extra cost.

**Dryland production budget, $/ac (regional analog):**

| Item | $/ac |
|---|---:|
| Seed (high-oleic hybrid) | 30 |
| Fertility | 70 |
| Herbicide program | 40 |
| Insecticide (head moth scouting) | 15 |
| Planting / spraying ops | 45 |
| Harvest (pan header or corn head adapt) | 40 |
| Crop insurance | 10 |
| **Total (before land)** | **250** |

*Irrigated adds ~$45/ac (water, extra fertility, harvest volume) → ~$295/ac.*

| Scenario | Math | Net $/ac before land |
|---|---|---:|
| Dryland · 1,600 lb | 16 cwt × $17.80 = $285 − $250 | **+$35** |
| Dryland + 45% oil premium | premium ≈ +$2.28/cwt ⇒ +$36/ac | **+$71** |
| Irrigated · 2,200 lb + premium | 22 cwt × $20.55 ≈ $452 − $295 | **+$157** |

**Honest read:**
- Base-case dryland **loses to a good soybean year** — sunflower recruits as rotation ground, marginal dryland, and irrigated acres chasing the premium.
- Agronomic sweeteners: deep taproot mines residual N and moisture; earlier harvest spreads combine hours; excellent rotation/double-crop fit behind wheat.
- **Minimum viable program: ~2,000 contracted acres (≈64 loads)** — below that, bin opportunity cost and procedure overhead aren't worth the elevator's trouble.

## 8 · The barge card

The base case trucks everything 600 miles west. But Lake Providence is a river terminal: if an export or Gulf-side buyer materializes — Mexico and EU birdseed demand both exist — it loads barges at roughly a quarter of the truck cost. **Only a river elevator can offer that, and no other river elevator is pitching sunflowers.**

| Outbound lane | $/cwt | Status |
|---|---:|---|
| Truck → Lubbock (base case) | $3.60 | estimate — needs spot quote |
| Barge → Gulf (Viserion-only) | ~$1.00 | typical lower-Mississippi oilseed rate — needs quote |

| **~$2.60/cwt** saved | **~$42/ac** to growers | **+$33/ac at $20 delivered** |
|---|---|---|
| freight advantage over any truck-based competitor | turns a marginal crop solid | barge keeps growers positive at the price where the truck program goes negative |

*Status: option, not base case — no Gulf/export buyer identified yet; finding one is part of Gate 2 and the winter-2027 review. Even unexercised, the option disciplines Lubbock's bid.*

## 9 · Where it breaks — and what we do about it

**Grower net $/ac (dryland, 16 cwt, no premium):**

| Delivered price | Truck ($3.60) | Barge ($1.00) |
|---|---:|---:|
| $20.00 | **−$9** | +$33 |
| $22.75 (current ref) | +$35 | +$77 |
| $25.00 | +$71 | +$113 |
| $28.00 | +$119 | +$161 |

**Freight sensitivity (at $22.75 delivered):**

| Truck spot quote | Grower net $/ac |
|---|---:|
| $3.00/cwt | +$45 |
| $3.60/cwt (model) | +$35 |
| $4.25/cwt (gate threshold) | +$25 |
| $4.50/cwt | +$21 |
| $5.00/cwt | +$13 |

Freight alone doesn't kill dryland — but stacked with a soft price it does: **$20 delivered + $4.50 freight = −$23/ac.** That combination is the kill condition.

**The honest case against — and mitigations:**
- **One-buyer risk.** Program realistically sells to Red River Lubbock first; the K-State arbitrage model assumed multiple reachable buyers. → Signed offtake before any bin commits; NSA directory worked for a second outlet; barge option as leverage.
- **Thin grower math** at base case — loses to good soybeans. → Oil premium, irrigated acres, rotation/marginal ground; AOG clause caps downside.
- **Price volatility:** $16.60→$31.40/cwt in one season (2021). → AOG contracts locked pre-plant, non-negotiable.
- **Freight is an extrapolation** past USDA's 200-mi data ceiling. → Gate 2 spot quote before term sheet.
- **Fire/insurance:** fines are a real dryer hazard. → Written daily-cleanout protocol; underwriter conversation early (Gate 3), not after a claim.
- **Small headline dollars** for a Pinnacle-backed platform ($26k–$128k). → The pitch is zero-risk fee income, slack-window utilization, grower originations in a parish Viserion already competes in, and barge option value.
- **Unresearched (not disproven):** permits, LDAF seed/feed licensing, labor, deer-plot retail pricing — scoped in the VAPG-funded study.

## 10 · Four gates before anyone commits a dollar

| # | Gate | What "pass" looks like | Kill condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Red River (Lubbock) offtake | Written commitment: delivered price, quality spec (oil %, moisture, FM), volume cap, AOG availability | No contract → no program. Full stop. |
| 2 | Real freight quotes | Truck spot ≤ ~$4.25/cwt; barge oilseed rate to NOLA quoted | Truck > $4.25 with no barge buyer identified |
| 3 | Ops + insurance sign-off | Underwriter accepts the written sunflower drying/fines protocol at no material premium change | Underwriting surprise or exclusion |
| 4 | Grower commitment | 2,000 acres of LOIs at modeled farmgate ($17.80/cwt base) | Growers won't plant → program dies regardless |

**Outside money that de-risks the study:**
- **USDA VAPG planning grant** — up to ~$75k, 1:1 match, funds exactly the professional feasibility study this plan needs; working-capital tranche (~$250k) later. Confirm current NOFA.
- **LDAF RFSI** — Louisiana awarded $4.6M+ in 2024 for middle-of-supply-chain processing/storage; fits the future food-oil pathway (not birdseed). Watch for the next round.
- **USDA REAP / LED rural programs** — standard complements for Structure C equipment.
