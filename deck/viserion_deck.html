The opportunity

Sunflowers grow here. Nobody buys them here.

The nearest buyer of any kind is ~600 road miles away. That single gap — no local delivery point — is what keeps a workable crop out of the Delta. Whoever becomes the delivery point owns the origination.

Deal structure

Three ways in — start where the risk is zero

2027 · Recommended

A — Toll handling

Growers hold act-of-God contracts directly with the buyer. Viserion never owns the seed — it charges receiving, drying, storage, and freight-coordination fees.

Viserion risk: near zero. Return: fee income on idle capacity.

2028

B — Merchandising

Viserion posts a local bid, owns the seed, sells delivered-Lubbock or via barge, and keeps basis + freight margin on top of fees.

Adds: historically $0.20–0.50/cwt where buyers exist. Costs: market risk in a thin, one-buyer market.

2029 · evaluate

C — Birdseed line JV

At ≥5,000 acres, add cleaning/bagging at the elevator — bagged black-oil birdseed and deer-plot seed sold regionally, erasing the freight penalty entirely.

Capital: $150–500k placeholder, pending written vendor quotes.

The honest case against — and mitigations
  • One-buyer risk. Program realistically sells to Red River Lubbock first. → Signed offtake before any bin commits; NSA directory for a second outlet.
  • Thin grower math at base case. → Oil premium, irrigated acres, rotation ground.
  • Freight is an extrapolation. A $4.50/cwt spot quote breaks dryland. → Go/no-go gate.
  • Insurance must sign off on dryer fire protocol. → Early underwriter conversation.
  • Small headline dollars for a Pinnacle-backed platform. → The pitch is zero-risk fees, slack-window utilization, originations, and barge option value.
