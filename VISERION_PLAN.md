# Viserion Grain × Delta Sunflower Program
## Partnership Proposal & Financial Model — Lake Providence, LA (East Carroll Parish)

**Prepared by:** William (Tap Parker Farms)
**Date:** August 3, 2026
**Builds on:** REPORT.md (July 2026 feasibility research)

---

## 1. The pitch in one paragraph

Viserion Grain's Lake Providence river elevator (acquired from Zen-Noh/Bunge in 2021) already has everything a sunflower program needs — scales, pits, dryers, segregated bins, truck and **barge** loadout — and most of it sits at partial utilization outside the corn/soybean rush. Sunflowers grown in the northeast Louisiana Delta have willing distant buyers (Red River Commodities' Lubbock, TX birdseed plant, ~600 mi; ND crushers via contract) but **no local delivery point**, which is the single thing killing the crop here. Viserion can become that delivery point for **modest incremental capital (~$25k–$75k)**, earn handling/drying/storage/freight fees on a new commodity, and — uniquely among anyone in this region — hold a **barge card**: if an export or Gulf-side buyer materializes, Lake Providence loads barges at roughly a quarter of the truck cost to Lubbock.

## 2. Why Viserion specifically

| Asset Viserion already has | What the program needs it for |
|---|---|
| Truck receiving, scales, grading | Delivery point for growers within ~50 mi |
| Grain dryers | Sunflower must hit 10% moisture (7–8% for summer carry) |
| Segregated storage bins | Identity-preserved high-oleic seed; keep fines-prone seed apart |
| Truck loadout | 500-cwt loads to Lubbock or NSA-directory buyers |
| **Barge loadout on the Mississippi** | Optionality: export/Gulf movement at ~$0.75–1.00/cwt vs ~$3.60/cwt truck |
| Merchandising desk | Contracting with Red River / crushers; basis and freight arbitrage |
| Early-season slack capacity | Early-planted Delta sunflowers harvest **Aug–early Sept**, ahead of the soybean/corn peak — the facility earns fees in an otherwise slow window |

The one genuinely new operational requirement is **fines management**: sunflower hairs/fibers are the fire risk in dryers (NDSU: it's housekeeping, not temperature). That is a daily-cleaning procedure change, not a capital project.

## 3. Three partnership structures (pick one to lead with)

**Structure A — Toll handling (fee-for-service). RECOMMENDED OPENER.**
Growers contract directly with the end buyer (act-of-God contracts with Red River or a crusher). Viserion never owns the seed — it charges receiving, drying, storage, and freight-coordination fees. Viserion's risk: near zero. Viserion's return: fee income on idle capacity.

**Structure B — Merchandising.**
Viserion posts a local bid, owns the seed, sells delivered-Lubbock (or barge/export), and keeps basis + freight margin on top of fees. Higher return, but Viserion carries price/market risk on a thin, one-buyer market. Sensible in year 2–3 once quality history and buyer relationships exist.

**Structure C — Joint-venture birdseed line (long game).**
Once volume proves out (≥5,000 ac equivalent), add a cleaning/bagging line at or near the elevator and sell bagged black-oil birdseed and deer-plot seed regionally — every bag sold locally avoids the entire $3.60/cwt freight penalty. Capital placeholder $150k–$500k, **quote-dependent** (all internet equipment prices were refuted in verification — see REPORT.md §5).

**Recommended sequence: A (2027) → B (2028) → evaluate C (2029).**

### 4.4 Grower economics per acre (this is what recruits acres)

Farmgate = $22.75 delivered − $3.60 freight − $1.35 fees = **$17.80/cwt**.

| | Dryland 1,600 lb | Dryland + 45% oil premium | Irrigated 2,200 lb + premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue/ac | $285 | $321 | $452 |
| Production cost/ac (seed, fert, chem, ops, harvest, ins) | ($250) | ($250) | ($295) |
| **Net/ac before land** | **+$35** | **+$71** | **+$157** |

### 4.5 Sensitivity — grower net $/ac (dryland 16 cwt, no oil premium)

| Delivered price → | $20.00 | $22.75 | $25.00 | $28.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Truck to Lubbock ($3.60 frt) | **−$9** | +$35 | +$71 | +$119 |
| Barge to Gulf ($1.00 frt) | +$33 | +$77 | +$113 | +$161 |

Two things jump out: (1) at sub-$20 delivered prices the truck program is underwater for growers — the program needs act-of-God contracts locked **before planting**; (2) **the barge lane is worth ~$42/ac** — it converts a marginal crop into a solid one, and only Viserion can offer it.

### 4.6 Minimum viable program

- **Floor:** ~2,000 contracted acres (≈64 loads). Below that, segregated-bin opportunity cost and procedure overhead aren't worth Viserion's trouble.
- **One bin test:** even a 100,000-bu bin holds ~30,000 cwt of sunflower (light test weight) — one bin covers the pilot.

## 5. How it WOULD work — the case for

1. **Near-zero capital, fee income on slack capacity** in an Aug–Sept window before the row-crop rush.
2. **Risk is structurable to zero** for Viserion in year 1 (toll model; growers hold AOG contracts with the buyer).
3. **Barge optionality is a real, exclusive edge** — a ~$2.60/cwt freight advantage over any truck-based competitor if an export/Gulf buyer is found.
4. **Oil premiums reward Delta agronomy:** high-oleic at 44–46% oil adds ~10% to price at no cost to the elevator.
5. Grows Viserion's grower relationships and originations in a parish where they compete for corn/beans anyway.

## 6. How it WOULDN'T work — the honest case against

1. **One-buyer risk.** Realistically the whole program sells to Red River Lubbock at first. If they pass or bid low, there is no arbitrage — the K-State elevator model assumed multiple reachable buyers. *Mitigation: signed offtake before any bin is committed; NSA buyer directory for a second outlet.*
2. **Thin grower math.** +$35/ac dryland base case loses to good soybean years. Sunflower only recruits acres as a rotation/marginal-ground/double-crop play or with the oil premium and irrigation. If growers won't plant 2,000 ac, the program dies regardless of Viserion's interest.
3. **Price volatility.** $16.60→$31.40/cwt in 2021 cuts both ways; a $20 delivered price puts truck-program growers negative (see 4.5).
4. **Freight estimate is an extrapolation.** USDA rates stop at 200 mi; if the real Lubbock spot quote comes in at $4.50/cwt, dryland economics break. This is a go/no-go number.
5. **Fire/insurance:** sunflower fines are a documented dryer-fire hazard. Manageable with daily cleaning, but Viserion's insurer must sign off — an underwriting surprise could add cost or kill it.
6. **Volume is small money for a company Viserion's size.** $25k–$128k contribution won't excite a Pinnacle-backed platform on its own — the pitch must lean on zero risk, slack-window utilization, origination relationships, and the barge/export option value.

## 7. Go/no-go gates (in order)

1. **Red River Commodities (Lubbock) offtake:** will they contract Delta-grown black-oil/high-oleic, at what delivered price and spec? *No contract → no program.*
2. **Real freight quotes:** Lake Providence→Lubbock truck spot; Lake Providence→NOLA barge for oilseed.
3. **Viserion insurance/ops sign-off** on sunflower drying protocol.
4. **2,000 acres of grower letters of intent** at the modeled farmgate price.
5. Re-pull current NSA bids (sunflowernsa.com) at contracting time — all prices here are planning numbers.

## 9. Caveats

Every dollar figure is a planning estimate: prices are late-2025 bids (still referenced mid-2026), freight beyond 200 mi is extrapolated, production budgets are regional analogs, and elevator cost curves are dated studies. The model's *structure* is the deliverable; gates 1–2 replace the two most fragile numbers with real ones before anyone commits capital or acres.
